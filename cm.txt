v13: make survival the objective, and find the fly was using the wrong half

Stated plainly - survive as long as possible - the numbers already on file said
the effort had gone into the wrong half of the pilot. Three baselines run to
death reframed it:

  hand-written kiter, no brain at all   216.0 s   wave 7.9   113 kills
  v7                                    156.5 s   wave 6.9    88 kills
  the same kiter, never shooting        114.9 s   wave 1.0     0 kills

The player moves at 185 px/s against a walker 52 and a devil 92, so nothing on
the map can catch a mover, and v7 was not using that: a repulsion rule with no
connectome in it beats it by 60 s and the whole gap is movement. The third row
is the other half - a kiter that never fires is stuck on wave 1 forever,
because the game only advances once the board is clear, so it accumulates
zombies until cornered. Killing here is crowd control and it is worth 101 s.

v13 splits the job: movement by repulsion (HAND), standoff radius from DNp01
rate (BRAIN), aim from the readout (BRAIN), trigger by line-of-fire geometry
(HAND).

  v13   197.4 +/- 47.0 s   wave 7.9   110.0 kills   42.7% hit

  v13 - v7      survival  +40.9 s  (t = +3.11)  better in 16/24
  v13 - v7      kills     +21.5    (t = +2.22)
  v13 - kiter   survival  -18.7 s  (t = -0.96)  level
  v13 - kiter   kills      -3.0    (t = -0.24)  level

v13 and the kiter have byte-identical legs and differ only in the eyes: the
kiter turns on the true bearing from the game, v13 on a linear readout of 1,314
descending neurons. That readout hits 42.7% where perfect aim hits 63.2% and it
costs nothing measurable in survival. Aim had stopped being the binding
constraint two versions earlier and nobody had checked.

Also in this commit:

Barrels are a setting now, Game(n_barrels=...), because they were asked to be
taken off the live map. server.py and play.py default to none; every benchmark
table stays at 7 so it remains comparable. Measured on v7: removing them is
worth +20.0 s of survival (t = +1.70) and +34.6 kills (t = +3.74).

Contact-range behaviour measured over 9,195 frames, because it looked from the
outside like the fly goes blind when something touches it. It does not: every
detector sits at or near its 0.8 ceiling and it is trying to flee on 80.7% of
those frames. What actually goes wrong is that the barrel rule gags the gun on
40% of them and it is facing 61 degrees off the thing touching it, so a rule
that reverses along the body axis reverses the wrong way. v13 movement does not
depend on facing.
