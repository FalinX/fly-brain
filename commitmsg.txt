v11 and v12: balance the aim data, and find out mean error was the wrong metric

v10 showed readouts here are fitted on data dominated by frames where the
answer does not matter. Aim was predicted, before the fit, to be the opposite
case: off-axis frames only need roughly-right, while nearly-centred frames are
where a bullet lands or misses, so oversampling off-axis should hurt. Both
resamplings were fitted and compared offline first.

  aim error by band       0-0.15  0.15-0.40  0.40-0.90  0.90-pi   side ok
  shipped (v7)             22.7       28.2       33.8      62.1     79.7%
  v11 bearing-uniform      59.6       51.7       49.1      47.5     79.7%
  v12 centre-weighted       5.5       11.5       29.1      98.3     79.0%

The prediction held both ways. v11, which is v10 winning trick applied to aim,
wrecks the decisive band and was never run. v12 is four times sharper there.

In the game v12 is far worse: survival -53.5 s (t = -3.73), kills -43.9
(t = -4.66), and hit rate -11.9 points (t = -5.01). A four times sharper
readout made the fly shoot worse.

Two explanations were tested and both are false. It does not fail to reach the
states it is accurate in: it sits inside 0.15 rad on 39.1 percent of frames
against v7 29.3 percent. It is not jumpier either. Measured on identical
states with v7 driving:

                    error centred   frame-to-frame   truth moves
  v7                    18.1 deg       13.1 deg       5.7 deg
  v12                    6.5 deg        2.9 deg

v12 is too slow, not too noisy. It is precise about where the target was and
cannot follow where it is going. Cross-validation caused it: concentrating the
fit into a narrow band left less to explain and it chose rank 50 against v7
rank 300. Lower rank is smoother and smoother is lag, so the centre-weighting
bought precision by buying smoothness.

Standing lesson, now in the README: mean angular error is a static metric on a
control signal, it cannot see bandwidth, and bandwidth is what a closed loop
runs on. No offline number in this repo was measuring it.
